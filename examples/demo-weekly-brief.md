# Northwind HR (demo): weekly demand-gen brief, 12 Oct 2026

_This example was written from the synthetic demo client's outputs. It shows the format and depth `/weekly-review` aims for._

## TL;DR
- **Non-brand organic clicks fell 43% (1,713 → 973)** while impressions rose 5%. Two causes: `/payroll` lost page 1 for its head terms, and AI Overviews are taking clicks on two informational guides.
- **Meta is producing cheap leads that don't become pipeline.** It has the lowest CPL (£41) and the worst Cost/SQL (£1,300), against £340 on LinkedIn.
- **This week:** £2.2k/month of zero-conversion search spend gets negated, budget moves from Meta prospecting to LinkedIn and Microsoft Ads, and the `/payroll` page gets defended with paid while it's fixed.

## Cross-channel insights
- **The SEO loss and the PPC win are the same keywords.** "payroll software", "payroll software uk" and "hr software" all convert in paid (33 conv.) and sit at organic positions 8–12. These are the highest-value SEO briefs on the account.
- **AI Overviews hit "what is an hris" (CTR −12 pts at position 2) and "how to run payroll".** Rankings held, so this isn't a ranking problem. Reframe these pages as assisted-conversion content. Meanwhile, AI referrals (chatgpt.com) produced only 18 leads but have the best MQL→SQL rate on the account (64%). Investing in GEO is justified.
- **Email nurture MQLs almost never become SQLs (1 of 20).** Either the MQL scoring is too generous for nurture engagement, or sales isn't working these leads. This is a question for sales ops, not a media problem.

## Risks & watch-list
- Spend exports cover September only, but the HubSpot cohort is Q3. Treat Cost/SQL as relative between channels until the windows are aligned.
- Brand search is paid while ranking #1 organically. That's a candidate for a 4-week holdout test; don't pause it.
